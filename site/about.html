About FaultLine Utah

Utah's earthquake information is good, but it's scattered across a half-dozen agencies, each speaking its own technical language. FaultLine Utah gathers the most useful parts, translates them into plain language for a specific address, and turns them into a short list of things to do.

We don't run our own hazard models. We present published, authoritative results from the Utah Geological Survey and the U.S. Geological Survey, and every result links to its source.

Our rules

  1. Translate, don't invent. We explain what the experts have published. We don't make new predictions.
  2. Every claim has a source. Each result card says where it came from.
  3. Bands, not false precision. Where the science is uncertain, we use plain categories, with the numbers one tap away under "Show the science."
  4. Experts review the science. We're recruiting a seismologist and a structural engineer to review this site's science and building guidance. Some features, like basin depth by address, stay off until they have.

Privacy

  • We don't save the addresses you look up. Your location stays in your browser's address bar after the #, a part browsers never send to a server.
  • To turn an address into a map point, your search text goes to UGRC's address locator, and to OpenStreetMap if UGRC can't find it. To get building-code values, a rounded location (about 100 m) goes to the USGS. The base map loads tiles for the area you're viewing.
  • Fault, liquefaction, and Magna results are computed in your browser from files on this site, so those lookups send your location to no one.
  • Make a Plan keeps your answers and checkmarks in this browser only. "Start over" erases them.
  • No ads, no tracking cookies, no analytics that identify you.

What this site is, and isn't

FaultLine Utah is for education and preparedness only. It is not an engineering assessment, inspection, insurance determination, real-estate disclosure, or earthquake prediction. Talk to a licensed structural or geotechnical engineer about any specific building or site.

Utah does not have a public earthquake early warning system yet, and this site never sends alerts. For emergencies, call 911. For official guidance, follow Be Ready Utah and your city or county emergency manager.

Our sister site

FaultLine Utah covers earthquakes in depth. For every other hazard, visit Prepare Utah. Answer a few questions about your home and household and get a personal preparedness plan for wildfire, outbreaks, drought, air quality, floods, power outages, and more, with water and food storage amounts.
